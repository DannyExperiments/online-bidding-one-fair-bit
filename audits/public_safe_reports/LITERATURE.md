# Q459 source and priority assessment

**Verdict.** The exact finite-uniform online-bidding result is a consequence of classical deterministic multi-problem contract scheduling. The normalized constant, optimal geometric merged sequence, and the prefix-ratio lower-bound functional were already known. The attribution and short transfer belong in any release. I found no earlier source explicitly applying that result to the one-fair-bit bidding question or stating the general finite uniform bidding theorem. Confidence in this last bounded search is moderate. An absolute priority claim is unwarranted.

## Source and exact objective

Spyros Angelopoulos and Bertrand Simon, *Learning-Augmented Online Bidding in Stochastic Settings*, NeurIPS 2025, §1 equation (1), §3 first paragraph and §6 final paragraph. A target u≥1 is fixed independently of the random seed. The bid sequence is increasing and positive. It stops at the first bid at least u and pays every bid through that one. The randomized objective is the supremum over targets of the expected cost divided by u. Section 6 asks for the best ratio with one random bit. The official proceedings and arXiv full text were checked. [Proceedings, pp. 2, 6, 10](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/618f396e2df006be25412505a793879a-Paper-Conference.pdf) · [ArXiv, conclusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25582#S6).

The result concerns standard online bidding without advice, a fixed target, and a uniform choice among k schedules. One fair bit supplies k=2. With at most b independent fair bits, unused bits can be read in advance and outcomes mapped to repeated schedules, so the case k=2^b covers early stopping and repeated schedules. The theorem makes no assertion about arbitrary weights on k schedules, adaptive selection of the target after seeing the seed, or optimizing with target advice.

## Exact prior implication

- Shlomo Zilberstein, François Charpillet and Philippe Chassaing, *Real-Time Problem-Solving with Contract Algorithms*, IJCAI 1999, pp. 1008–1013, §3.2 Lemma 2 and Theorem 3 (printed p. 1011, PDF p. 4), and Appendix A. In their normalization the omniscient benchmark divides time equally among k instances. The optimum is exactly (1+1/k)^(k+1). Their geometric merged lengths use the same base 1+1/k. Lemma 2 gives the prefix functional with the k normalization. [Primary PDF, p. 4](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1999/0144.pdf#page=4).
- Daniel S. Bernstein, Lev Finkelstein and Shlomo Zilberstein, *Contract Algorithms and Robots on Rays: Unifying Two Scheduling Problems*, IJCAI 2003, pp. 1211–1217, Definition 1, Lemma 1 and Theorems 1–2. Their benchmark runs the one requested problem, so for one processor and k problem types the optimal cyclic acceleration is k(1+1/k)^(k+1). Definitions start the guarantee after one contract per type has finished. The lower bound applies to cyclic schedules, which is sufficient for the transfer. The specialized recurrence in their Lemma 2 is the same prefix inequality. [Primary PDF, pp. 3–4](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2003/0172.pdf#page=3). The single-problem multiprocessor AAAI 2002 paper is a different theorem.
- Spyros Angelopoulos and Alejandro López-Ortiz, *Interruptible Algorithms for Multiproblem Solving*, IJCAI 2009 and [arXiv:1810.11291, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11291#S2), identify the bidding equivalence in the single-type, single-processor deterministic case. Their later discussion distinguishes normalized objectives and credits the broader classical scheduling results. I found no finite-uniform k>1 bidding transfer in that text.

**Transfer checked here.** Discard bids below 1 and merge the schedules into the nondecreasing list c₁,c₂,… with prefix sums S. For any target u let j count entries below u. The original total charge is at least S_(j+k). Assigning this list cyclically to k seeds attains that prefix charge at every u, including u=1 and ties. At a fixed value c_j, take the right limit after the final copy of that value. A competitive ratio R gives S_(j+k)≤kR c_j also for earlier tied positions. If a scheduling theorem requires strict lengths, use c'_j=(1+ε(1−2^(-j)))c_j. Then S'_(j+k)/c'_j≤(1+ε)kR. A cyclic contract schedule running these lengths consecutively has acceleration sup_j S'_(j+k)/c'_j, since just before completion j+k the minimum latest length across types is c'_j. The formula starts after all types have a completed contract. The classical lower bound followed by ε→0 gives the bidding lower bound. Deleting any finite prefix and recoloring gives the same lower bound on each tail supremum. Omitted startup work is a fixed additive numerator over c_j→∞, so this also gives the limsup lower bound. The standalone logarithmic proof from the packet gives the same inequality and limit.

## Related settings

- Marek Chrobak, Claire Kenyon, John Noga and Neal E. Young, *Incremental Medians via Online Bidding*, Algorithmica 50 (2008), 455–478, Theorems 1–2 and §2. The classical deterministic ratio is 4 and unrestricted randomized ratio is e. The latter upper bound samples a continuous log phase. Their lower bound applies to all randomization, but does not sharpen e for k fixed equally likely choices. [Author-hosted journal version](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~neal/publication/Chrobak08Online.pdf).
- Angelopoulos, Arsénio and Kamali, *Competitive Sequencing with Noisy Advice*, arXiv:2111.05281, §4 Theorems 5 and 7. Geometrically interleaved schedules and contract-scheduling reductions are established in this setting. Correct advice selects a favorable schedule depending on the target; adversarial advice calls for a per-schedule robustness guarantee; noisy advice changes the selection rule. These are different objectives from the mean over uniform seed outcomes. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05281). The general consistency/robustness tradeoff also appears in [*Online Computation with Untrusted Advice*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05655).
- Angelopoulos, Bienkowski, Dürr and Simon, *Contract Scheduling with Distributional and Multiple Advice*, IJCAI 2024, §§2–4, use distributions on interruption times and sets of predictions. The advice comes from information about the input. It is not an internal uniform seed available on every target. [Proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/404) · [Full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12485).
- Degryse, Saakour, Dürr and Angelopoulos, *The Pareto Frontier of Randomized Learning-Augmented Online Bidding*, arXiv:2605.06106v2 (May 2026), §3 Theorem 2 and §§4–6. Their bidding-function representation uses a continuous uniform offset and addresses consistency under target predictions versus worst-case robustness. Their discretized proof/LP does not constrain the number of random bits. [Full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06106v2).
- Kraakman, Krekelberg, H.-H. Liu and F.-H. Liu, *A Primal-Dual Approach to Randomized Online Bidding with Tail Constraints*, arXiv:2610.05580 (October 2026), §4 periodic strategies. Tail-exceedance constraints are probability bounds on individual losses. Their phase optimization is over continuous offset distributions. Neither is a finite equiprobable support restriction. [Full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05580).
- *Randomized Algorithms for Online Bounded Bidding* bounds the number of recent bids whose sum is charged. This parameter changes the cost function and is not a limit on the seed. [Primary author PDF, definition and Table 1](https://math.haifa.ac.il/lea/bounded_bidding.pdf). Linear and star search can involve a partial final traversal; Angelopoulos–Simon’s conclusion separately names linear search. The present result is confined to the stated bidding cost.

The bounded search used the source question, one-bit and finite-support terminology, the exact constant, known contract-scheduling links and the later bidding and advice references above. No earlier explicit finite-uniform bidding transfer was found.
